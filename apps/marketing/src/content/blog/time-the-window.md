Small creators do not usually pick bad niches. They pick good niches late.

By the time a niche reaches you organically, through a video essay about it or
a screenshot in a Discord, it has already been discovered, copied, and crowded.
You heard about it because it peaked. Entering at that moment means fighting
channels with a two year head start for an audience that is already split.
The niche was real. Your timing was the problem.

Timing is fixable, but only if you can see where a niche sits in its lifecycle.
There are three stages, and only one of them wants you.

## Stage one: Emerging

Somewhere on YouTube, a small channel's recent uploads start accelerating past
its own baseline. Not big numbers yet. Just a slope that was flat and suddenly
is not. Almost nobody else runs the niche.

This is the highest risk entry. The niche is unproven, and plenty of
accelerating channels flatten out and die quietly. But if it lands, you were
first, and first movers in a niche collect the subscriptions everyone later
fights over. Enter here only if you can afford to be wrong.

## Stage two: Breaking Out

The niche has now cleared the proof bar: regular uploads earn serious median
views, not one lucky viral. Momentum is strong. And, critically, only a handful
of channels run the play.

This is the window. Demand is confirmed, supply has not caught up, and the
algorithm is actively hunting for more of this content to feed a hungry
audience. A competent clone entering now rides the same wave that is lifting
the original. Every week you deliberate, someone else enters. When a niche is
breaking out, the clock is running.

## Stage three: Established

Two ways a niche lands here, and both close the door.

Either it got **crowded**: twenty channels running the same format, the
audience split twenty ways, discovery a coin flip. Or it **cooled**: the
channels are still there but the acceleration is gone, views flat or sliding.

Here is the rule that surprises people: **crowding beats momentum.** A niche
can still be growing fast and be a terrible entry, because that growth is
already spoken for by channels ahead of you in every recommendation queue. Big
view counts in an established niche are not opportunity. They are someone
else's moat.

## How to read momentum yourself

Momentum is always measured against a channel's **own baseline**, never against
the platform. A cooking channel jumping from 20K median views to 45K is a
louder signal than a gaming channel coasting at 800K.

The manual version: for a channel's recent uploads, divide views by video age
in days to get a rough daily rate. Compare that rate to the same figure for
uploads from a few months back. If recent videos earn views two or three times
faster than the channel's norm, it is accelerating. If the rate is flat or
falling, the exciting part already happened.

Doing this for one channel takes ten minutes. Doing it for every candidate
niche, every week, so you catch the move from emerging to breaking out while
the window is still open, is the part no working creator has time for.

The niche you are considering right now is in one of these three stages. Before
you script a single video, be honest about which one.

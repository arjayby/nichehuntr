Small creators do not usually pick bad niches. They pick good niches late.

By the time a niche reaches you organically, through a video essay about it or
a screenshot in a Discord, it has already been discovered and copied. You heard
about it because it peaked. Entering at that moment means fighting channels
with a two year head start for an audience they have already banked. The niche
was real. Your timing was the problem.

Timing is fixable, but only if you can see where a niche sits in its lifecycle.
There are three stages, and only one of them wants you.

## Stage one: Emerging

Somewhere on YouTube, a small channel is uploading Shorts every few days, and
most of its recent ones are already clearing 50K views each. Not huge numbers.
Just a floor that channels going nowhere never reach, showing up again and
again while the channel is still tiny.

This is the highest risk entry. The niche has a pulse, not proof, and plenty of
channels clear the small bar for a month and then die quietly. But if it lands,
you were first, and first movers in a niche collect the subscriptions everyone
later fights over. Enter here only if you can afford to be wrong.

## Stage two: Breaking Out

The niche has now cleared the real bar: most of the channel's latest Shorts
are earning 100K views or more, upload after upload, and the uploads are still
fresh. Not one lucky viral. A repeatable format printing six figure views
while the channel behind it is still small. The audience is arriving faster
than the channel can bank it.

This is the window. Demand is confirmed, the head start is not yet built, and
the algorithm is actively hunting for more of this content to feed a hungry
audience. A competent clone entering now rides the same wave that is lifting
the original. Every week you deliberate, the incumbent banks more of the
audience. When a niche is breaking out, the clock is running.

## Stage three: Established

The channel still clears the 100K bar on recent Shorts. The difference is what
it has behind it now: 50K or more subscribers and a catalog dozens of Shorts
deep. The wave did not die. It got claimed.

Established is not a graveyard, and it is not a signal the niche stopped
working. It is the opposite. It is the strongest proof on the board that the
niche pays. But the surprise money was made a year ago. Entering now means
outperforming an incumbent who owns the watch history, the subscribers, and
the algorithm's trust in this format. Study an established channel. Learn its
format cold. Just know that you are not catching a wave anymore; you are
racing someone who already finished.

## How to run the read yourself

The whole lifecycle comes down to numbers you can check in five minutes. Open
a channel's Shorts tab, take the latest ten, and count how many cleared 100K
views. Five or more, with fresh uploads and a channel that is still small:
breaking out. The same reach on a channel with 50K+ subscribers and a deep
catalog: established. Most clearing only 50K: emerging, maybe.

Doing this for one channel takes five minutes. Doing it across every candidate
niche, week after week, so you catch the move from emerging to breaking out
while the window is still open, is the part no working creator has time for.

you script a single video, be honest about which one.
